# Day 3 (4 hours total - 3.5 hours coding, 0.5 hours reviewing numpy functions): I finished up physics.py tonight and got it completely up and running. It took quite a bit of time and reminders for certain numpy functions (roughly an extra 30 minutes to review some numpy functions; thankfully I have some experience with numpy already from a previous project). I will say that this was by far my favorite part of the project, so I'm a little sad to be done with it. Physics.py has 5 functions in total. The first, ballesteros_temperature calculation(), finds the surface temperature of a star in kelvins (using a B-V color index). The second function, absolute_magnitude_calculation(), does as the name implies: it calculates the absolute magnitude of a star using its apparent magnitude and distance/ parallax. The next function, parallax_to_distance(), simply converts the parallax units to parsecs. Finally, luminosity_calculation() calculates the luminosity of a star based on its absolute magnitude, and radius_calculation() calculates the star's radius in solar radii based off of its luminosity and temperature. I also completed the test script for physics.py, creating test functions for every singly function in physics.py (including testing their potential errors). That's all the technical stuff. In truth, today was a big physics learning day for me. I learned each of the equations I would need to efficiently create my project.
